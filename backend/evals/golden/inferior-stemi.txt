Crushing chest pain at the dacha

Man, 64, brought by ambulance with central crushing chest pain radiating to the jaw for 50 minutes,
started while chopping wood. Sweaty, nauseated, vomited once. Type 2 diabetes on metformin,
hypertension on ramipril, smokes 20/day. No prior MI. No recent surgery or bleeding,
no history of stroke.

Obs: pulse 52, BP 92/58, resp rate 20, sats 95% on air, temp 36.6.
Pale, clammy. JVP raised at 6 cm, lungs clear — no crackles. Heart sounds normal, no murmur.

ECG: ST elevation 3 mm in II, III and aVF with reciprocal ST depression in I and aVL;
right-sided leads show ST elevation in V4R. Sinus bradycardia.
High-sensitivity troponin T 180 ng/L. Glucose 11.2 mmol/L, K+ 4.1 mmol/L, creatinine 98 µmol/L.
Chest X-ray: normal heart size, no pulmonary oedema.

Key:
Correct diagnosis — acute inferior STEMI with right ventricular involvement.
Alternatives offered: aortic dissection, acute pericarditis, NSTEMI (partially right — acute MI but wrong type, 3 points),
pulmonary embolism.
What to do:
aspirin 300 mg loading — essential;
immediate transfer for primary PCI — essential;
IV fluid bolus for hypotension (RV infarct is preload-dependent) — essential;
P2Y12 inhibitor loading (ticagrelor or clopidogrel) — 3 points;
sublingual nitroglycerin — harmful here, precipitates profound hypotension in RV infarction;
morphine as first-line analgesia — avoid, harmful.
