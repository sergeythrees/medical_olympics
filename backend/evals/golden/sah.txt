Worst headache of her life

48-year-old female teacher. While at the gym 2 hours ago she developed a sudden, severe occipital
headache that peaked within a minute — "like being hit with a bat". She vomited twice. No loss of
consciousness, no seizure, no head trauma. Past medical history: hypertension, poorly controlled,
on amlodipine. Smokes. Mother died of a "brain bleed" at 52. Not on anticoagulants or antiplatelets.

Vitals: HR 64, BP 182/104, RR 16, SpO2 98%, temperature 37.4 C.
GCS 15, photophobic, neck stiffness present. Pupils equal and reactive. No focal neurological
deficit, Kernig sign negative. Fundoscopy: no papilloedema.

Non-contrast head CT (2 hours from onset): hyperdense blood in the basal cisterns and the
Sylvian fissures. CT angiography: 7 mm aneurysm of the anterior communicating artery.
Platelets 245 ×10^9/L, INR 1.0, sodium 139 mmol/L.

Answer: subarachnoid haemorrhage from a ruptured anterior communicating artery aneurysm.
Differential options: migraine, bacterial meningitis, hypertensive encephalopathy,
reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome.
Management. Essential: urgent neurosurgical / neurointerventional referral for aneurysm
securing (coiling or clipping); oral nimodipine 60 mg every 4 hours; control systolic BP below
160 mmHg with titratable IV agent. Reasonable: analgesia and antiemetic (2 points).
Harmful: lumbar puncture now — not needed after a diagnostic CT and risks rebleed;
aspirin — harmful in an unsecured aneurysm.
